# Empirical Distribution of Annualized Returns in US Public Equities

## Executive Summary
This document establishes the empirical research foundation and quantitative benchmark distributions used for quality control verification of our Investment Thesis Agent.

A central premise of financial markets is that individual equity returns are **heavily positively skewed** with fat right tails. While aggregate market indices generate positive real returns, the median individual stock substantially underperforms broad benchmarks, and only a distinct minority of equities achieve high-hurdle compound annual growth rates (CAGR >= 20%).

Comparing the distribution of forward annualized returns generated by our Investment Thesis Agent against established historical distributions provides a deterministic quality control test to detect systemic modeling biases, ungrounded multiple expansions, or unrealistic growth projections.

## Empirical Research Foundations

### 1. Hendrik Bessembinder & CRSP Database (1926-2023)
In the seminal paper *"Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?"* (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018, with continuous extensions through 2023), Professor Hendrik Bessembinder analyzed the lifetime and multi-period holding returns of over 26,000 common stocks listed on the NYSE, NASDAQ, and AMEX since 1926.

Key Findings:
- **Extreme Positive Skewness**: Across all individual US common stocks from 1926 to 2016, the cross-sectional distribution of 1-year returns exhibited a mean of +14.74% but a median of only +5.23%.
- **Concentration of Wealth Creation**: The best-performing 4.0% of listed companies accounted for all net wealth creation in the US stock market in excess of one-month Treasury bills. The remaining 96% of stocks collectively matched the return of one-month Treasury bills.
- **T-Bill Underperformance**: Over lifetime horizons, 58.4% of common stocks failed to beat the return of one-month Treasury bills, and the modal lifetime return was -100%.
- **Horizon Compounding Effect**: As holding periods lengthen (from 1 year to 3, 5, 10, and 20 years), the right-tail skewness compounds exponentially. High compounders (CAGR >= 20%) represent a shrinking percentage of the total equity population.

### 2. J.P. Morgan Asset Management: "The Agony & The Ecstasy"
Michael Cembalest's longitudinal study across the Russell 3000 universe (1980-2020) demonstrated the cross-sectional risk/return dynamics of individual common stocks:
- **Catastrophic Loss Rate**: Approximately 40% of all equities in the Russell 3000 suffered a permanent, unrecovered decline of 70% or more from peak value.
- **Underperformance vs Benchmark**: Roughly two-thirds (66%) of all stocks underperformed the broad Russell 3000 Index over their lifetime.
- **Extreme Winners**: Only ~7% of companies generated excess returns greater than two standard deviations above the market mean.

### 3. Large-Cap / Index Constituent Distribution (S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, DJIA)
For our actively tracked universe (144 constituents of QQQ, DIA, and SMH), historical constituent datasets show higher survivorship and profitability compared to microcaps:
- **3-Year Median CAGR**: Typically spans 7.0% to 9.0%.
- **Negative 3-Year Return Rate**: Approximately 20% to 25% of large caps experience negative returns over 3-year windows during normal market cycles.
- **High Compounder Rate (>=20% CAGR)**: Across large-cap universes, between 10% and 15% of constituents achieve >=20% annualized ROI over rolling 3-year horizons, primarily concentrated in technology, healthcare, and consumer platform leaders.

## Mathematical Properties of the Return Distribution

1. **Arithmetic vs Geometric Divergence**:
   $$\text{CAGR} = \left(\prod_{t=1}^{T} (1 + R_t)\right)^{1/T} - 1 \approx \mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$$
   Because individual stock volatility ($\sigma$) is high (~30% to 50% annualized for typical equities), the compound geometric growth rate (CAGR) is mathematically lower than the simple arithmetic average return ($\mu$).

2. **Extreme Positive Skewness ($\gamma_1 > 0$)**:
   $$\gamma_1 = \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(R_i - \bar{R})^3}{\sigma^3}$$
   Equity returns have a lower bound of -100% (loss of invested principal), but theoretically unbounded upside (+100%, +500%, +1,000%+). Consequently, a standard normal distribution is an invalid assumption; returns follow a right-skewed log-normal or power-law distribution.

3. **Fat Tails / Excess Kurtosis ($\kappa > 3$)**:
   Outsized returns are concentrated in a tiny fraction of companies that achieve network effects, platform dominance, or secular technology leadership (e.g., Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon).

## Quality Control Application to Investment Thesis Verification

The Investment Thesis Agent models 3-year forward price targets, revenue growth, and options harvest yields for each universe constituent. To verify that our models are realistic and grounded, the generated forward ROI distribution must satisfy specific acceptance bounds:

1. **Median Plausibility Bound**: The median annualized ROI of the universe should reside between **4.0% and 10.0%**. A median above 15% indicates systemic optimism or failure to model multiple compression.
2. **High-Hurdle Selectivity Bound (>=20% CAGR)**: Exactly **5% to 15%** of the universe should qualify for a BUY rating (>=20% CAGR). If 40% or 50% of the universe qualifies as BUY, the valuation bar is too loose.
3. **Downside / Negative Plausibility Bound**: Between **10% and 30%** of universe equities must exhibit negative or negligible returns (<0% or SELL/AVOID), reflecting cyclically overvalued or structurally challenged businesses.
4. **Rating-ROI Concordance**:
   - `BUY`: Strictly $\ge 20.0\%$ CAGR with positive margin of safety.
   - `HOLD`: $10.0\% - 19.9\%$ CAGR (augmented by Covered Calls).
   - `SELL`: $0.0\% - 9.9\%$ CAGR.
   - `AVOID`: $< 0.0\%$ CAGR or speculative structural impairment.
