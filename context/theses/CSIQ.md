## Business Profile
Canadian Solar Inc. is one of the world's largest solar technology and renewable energy companies, manufacturing solar photovoltaic modules and utility-scale battery energy storage systems (Recurrent Energy and e-STORAGE). The company operates vertically integrated ingot, wafer, cell, and module production facilities across North America, Asia, and Latin America.

Canadian Solar benefits from global manufacturing scale, advanced N-type TOPCon solar cell technology, and a massive utility-scale project development pipeline with long-term power purchase agreements. Management is rapidly scaling its high-margin turnkey battery energy storage solutions (e-STORAGE) to capitalize on the global grid-scale storage boom.

## Total Addressable Market & Market Share
The modeled addressable market combines utility and distributed solar modules, grid-scale battery systems, and renewable project development. ## Competitive Moat Analysis
Canadian Solar possesses a manufacturing scale and project development moat in solar photovoltaic modules and utility-scale battery energy storage systems. Vertically integrated solar manufacturing facilities across North America and Asia combined with advanced N-type TOPCon cell technology provide module cost efficiencies. Its e-STORAGE and Recurrent Energy project development arms hold multi-gigawatt utility contract pipelines with guaranteed long-term power purchase agreements.

## Revenue Drivers Narrative
The forecast is driven by three separable engines: module shipment volume and average selling prices, e-STORAGE backlog conversion, and Recurrent Energy project sales plus recurring electricity and service revenue. The 2025 mix shift toward storage lifted consolidated gross margin to 18.3% despite module-price pressure. The modeled path stays conservative because module oversupply, tariffs, project sale timing, and financing availability can easily offset storage growth in any single quarter.

## Valuation & P/S Multiple Narrative
A discounted sales multiple is appropriate because consolidated revenue combines cyclical, low-margin module manufacturing with more attractive but execution-heavy storage and project businesses. The existing model assumes only 3% annual revenue growth and a 25% contraction in the current price-to-sales multiple. That framing requires returns to come from backlog conversion and durable margin improvement, not from multiple expansion; failure to fund commitments or maintain gross margin above the Stage 1 floor invalidates the valuation case.
